Object Permanence Games for Babies: Peekaboo and Beyond

When a toy disappears under a cloth and your baby looks for it, a small game becomes a big idea: people and objects can still exist when they are out of sight.

Object permanence develops gradually. Early on, your baby may lose interest when a toy is hidden. Later, they may search, lift the cloth, or look toward where you went. These changes unfold through ordinary repetition, not special training.

Start with your face and voice

Peekaboo works because it is simple and social. Cover your face briefly with your hands, then return with a smile and a familiar phrase. Keep it slow enough for your baby to follow and stop while they are still enjoying it.

  • Hide a large soft toy partly under a cloth.
  • Let your baby watch where you place it.
  • Celebrate looking or reaching without turning it into a test.

Use everyday moments

Put a spoon in a cup, place a familiar toy in a basket, or let your baby watch you walk behind a doorway and come back. These tiny repetitions help the world feel more predictable. A baby who is not interested today may love the same game next week.

For more play that follows your baby’s curiosity, see our guide to baby vision development.

Keep it safe and low-pressure

Use only large, clean, age-appropriate toys and light cloths while you are present. Do not cover your baby’s face or leave fabrics nearby without supervision. If you are concerned about your baby’s play, communication, or loss of skills, bring those observations to a pediatric visit.
